## A note on the two Schwartz scales, because they get mixed up

Eugene Schwartz gave us two different ladders and the content lane needs both kept straight.

**Market sophistication** is a property of the market, not a person. It answers: how many times has this market heard a claim like ours? Santa Cruz gym buying sits at **Stage 3, the Mechanism Introducer**. Claims have been enlarged into noise, skepticism is high, and no local competitor has introduced a mechanism yet. There is one sophistication stage for the whole market.

**Prospect awareness** is a property of the individual and it is the one that varies by segment. Five levels: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware. Each segment below is tagged with its awareness level. That tag determines where the copy has to start for that person.

The rule: **sophistication sets the angle, awareness sets the starting line.** The mechanism, See Before You Sign, stays constant for everyone. How much you have to explain before you get to it changes by segment.

---

## Segment 1: The Serious Lifter

**Awareness: Most Aware, occasionally Product Aware.**

Already trains. Powerlifting or bodybuilding. Owns their programming. Currently at a chain they tolerate, driving out of the area, or lifting in a garage.

**What they actually want to know, in order:** what bars do you have, how many racks and platforms, is chalk allowed, how crowded is it at 6pm, what does it cost. That is the entire decision.

**What they do not need:** motivation, transformation language, an explanation of what strength training is, or a pitch about the community.

**Copy starting line:** the equipment list and the price. Skip the setup entirely. They are Most Aware, so the only job left is the offer and the specifics.

**Their voice, from a published review:** "Hands down best gym in Santa Cruz if you are serious about powerlifting or bodybuilding. Open 24/7, with everything you need to get stronger."

**Best offer for them:** the $20 day pass. They will judge the floor in forty minutes and decide. A tour is almost insulting to this segment, let them lift.

**Site path:** the experienced strength athlete starting point.

## Segment 2: The Returning Lifter

**Awareness: Problem Aware, and burned.**

Trained seriously at some point. Life happened. Has paid for at least one membership they did not use and probably had a bad time cancelling it. Wants back in and does not trust the process of joining.

This is the highest-intent, highest-skepticism segment in the market and the one the position was built for.

**Their real objection:** not "is this a good gym." It is "am I about to sign something I will regret." Every gym they have joined asked for commitment before proof.

**Copy starting line:** the mechanism, immediately. Free tour, no commitment. Day pass. Flex month-to-month with no agreement. 30-day cancellation stated up front. Lead with the terms, because the terms are the reassurance.

**Best offer for them:** Flex at $120/mo with no agreement, framed as the honest option rather than the expensive one. They will often migrate to a 12-month tier later once they trust it, which is a better lifetime value than pressuring them into an annual on day one.

**What kills this segment instantly:** any urgency tactic, any hidden fee discovered later, any pressure at the desk.

## Segment 3: The First-Time Barbell Lifter

**Awareness: Solution Aware, sometimes only Problem Aware.**

Knows they want to get stronger. Does not know what a rack is for, does not know how to start, and is genuinely afraid of looking stupid in a room full of people who clearly know what they are doing.

This is the segment nobody in Santa Cruz is openly serving. Chains take them and put them on machines. Strength gyms tacitly assume they already lift.

**Their real objection:** not price, not equipment. It is "will I be out of place there." The barrier is social, and it is the reason they have been putting this off for months.

**Copy starting line:** the staffing, then the tour. Someone is here Mon to Fri 8am to 7pm and Sat and Sun 9am to 2pm. Personal training is available and optional. The free tour is explicitly permission to look without deciding.

**Their voice, from a published review:** "I was greeted with the most amazing community. The people here are kind, supportive, and always happy to spot you and give tips."

That review is the single most valuable asset for this segment. It answers their actual question, will people be nice to me, in the words of a member rather than the gym.

**Best offer for them:** the free facility tour with no commitment. Never open with the day pass for this segment, walking in alone to train is the exact thing they are afraid of.

**Site path:** the first-time lifter starting point.

---

## Segment 4: The Constrained Schedule Lifter

**Awareness: Solution Aware.**

Weekend lifters, shift workers, parents, couples. They believe in training. They have done the math on a full-price membership against two sessions a week and concluded it is not worth it. That conclusion is why they are not a member anywhere.

**Their real objection:** value per visit. They are not price sensitive in the abstract, they are usage-rate sensitive.

**Copy starting line:** the tier that fits them, by name. Weekend Warrior from $45/mo. Couples from $120/mo. Then 24/7 access as the thing that makes an odd schedule workable at all.

**Best offer for them:** Weekend Warrior 12-month at $45/mo, or Couples 12-month at $120/mo for two people training together.

## Segment Priority

For the internal target of roughly 25 qualified leads per month:

1. **Segment 2, the Returning Lifter.** Highest intent, position built for them, shortest path from ad to tour.
2. **Segment 1, the Serious Lifter.** Smallest pool, highest retention, cheapest to reach because they are actively searching. Also the segment that makes the gym's reputation.
3. **Segment 3, the First-Time Barbell Lifter.** Largest pool, longest sales cycle, needs the most content. This is where the content lane earns its keep.
4. **Segment 4, the Constrained Schedule Lifter.** Real and underserved, lowest revenue per member. Worth a dedicated page, not worth the homepage.

## Language Rules Across All Segments

Pulled from the three published member reviews, which are the only voice-of-customer evidence that exists. Use their vocabulary, not marketing vocabulary.

**Words members actually use:** serious, everything you need, get stronger, roomy, open, accepting, kind, supportive, spot you, give tips, perfect location.

**Words to keep out of copy:** journey, transform, unleash, unlock, potential, elevate, state of the art, world class, fitness family, crush it, no excuses.

**The tone test:** would a member say this out loud to a friend in the parking lot. If not, cut it.

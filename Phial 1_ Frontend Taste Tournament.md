Turn the target website into a deliberate design tournament before committing to a visual direction. Preserve project truth while making the design search space visible: AI generates genuinely different possibilities, specialist skills sharpen them, and human comparative judgment decides what survives.

Begin with reconnaissance, not code. Inspect the existing repository, application structure, routes, framework, component system, dependencies, design tokens, fonts, reusable primitives, responsive conventions, real brand assets, approved photography, current copy, forms, navigation, integrations, and any existing implementation the user has already accepted. Distinguish project truth from disposable presentation. Preserve real content, functional behavior, brand commitments, URLs, form semantics, and approved assets unless the user explicitly asks to change them. For an existing site, understand what is being redesigned before touching it; for greenfield work, establish the smallest viable foundation without inventing product facts.

Before frontend implementation, establish the mandatory specialist stack. Locate and read the actual installed skill definitions rather than merely mentioning their names:

Read the relevant skill files fully before acting. Report which specialists were found, which files/processes were loaded, and what each owns in this phase. Do not claim a skill was used merely because it was installed: its characteristic process or checks must become visible in the work.

Hold this authority order throughout:

\*\*existing project truth and explicit human decisions → business intent and guardrails → reference-derived aesthetic evidence → specialist recommendations.\*\*

Skills advise and execute; they do not silently overrule established truth.

Now establish Chase's four anchors:

\*\*Aesthetic\*\* — define the candidate visual territory with a named family plus roughly 5–8 discriminating vocabulary terms that describe composition, typography, spatial rhythm, image treatment, materiality, density, color behavior, and atmosphere. Prefer concrete design language over adjectives such as “premium,” “modern,” or “cool.”

\*\*Reference\*\* — inspect the supplied screenshots, URLs, sites, or inspiration assets themselves before designing. Extract hierarchy, scale relationships, negative space, grid logic, section rhythm, typography, image framing, processing, surface character, motion posture, and other transferable principles. Match the \*\*feel and design grammar, not the content or exact composition\*\*.

\*\*Intent\*\* — state what the page is, who it serves, what the visitor should understand or feel within the opening seconds, the primary conversion action, and the hierarchy that should move them toward it.

\*\*Guardrails\*\* — derive project-specific \*\*Always\*\* constants and \*\*Never\*\* boundaries. Always rules encode identity and useful constraints. Never rules crowd out recurring AI defaults and brand violations. Preserve accessibility, legibility, responsive behavior, truthful content, and functional UX rather than treating anti-slop as permission for visual theatrics.

Use Taste Skill v2 to interpret those anchors and construct \*\*five genuinely different design worlds\*\* for the same underlying product, content truth, intent, conversion goal, and guardrails. Five is the default because comparison needs range; adjust only when the problem clearly requires fewer or more. Make the variance architectural rather than cosmetic: alter composition, scale, information density, navigation posture, section rhythm, typography system, spatial logic, body architecture, surface treatment, image logic, interaction character, and hierarchy. A palette swap does not qualify as another direction. Each candidate must commit strongly to itself; do not average the five aesthetics together.

Before implementing each direction, articulate:

\*\*Direction Name\*\*    
\*\*Aesthetic\*\* — family \+ vocabulary    
\*\*Reference Read\*\* — what source was inspected and what principles transfer    
\*\*Design Thesis\*\* — the one sentence governing the page    
\*\*Future Hero\*\* — the kind of visual that ultimately belongs here    
\*\*Hero Placement\*\* — exact relationship among image, focal point, headline, CTA, negative space, and responsive crop    
\*\*Body Grammar\*\* — how content, proof, features, conversion moments, and sections behave below the fold    
\*\*Signature Move\*\* — one memorable but defensible design decision    
\*\*Primary Risk\*\* — where this direction could become gimmicky, inaccessible, inappropriate, or generic

Keep final hero generation out of this phase. Reserve the hero composition precisely as though its final asset already existed: establish aspect ratio, focal zone, text-safe space, crop behavior, viewport relationship, and responsive placement, then use a restrained project-native stand-in. Approved real imagery may remain. Do not waste high-fidelity asset generation on directions that may die after comparison.

Build the five directions in clean isolation appropriate to the stack: routes, folders, branches, or another reversible scheme. Preserve common content and functional requirements so the user is comparing design rather than five unrelated products.

Use Emil's \`prototype\` approach to create a \*\*single visual comparison surface\*\* that makes the five directions easy to flip through or inspect together. Eliminate terminal-memory design: the user should not have to remember what version three looked like after version five appears. Where a component itself contains an unresolved high-leverage decision, Emil may prototype alternatives, but keep Phase 1 focused on macro-direction rather than polishing buttons while the entire aesthetic is still undecided.

Then independently red-team every candidate with Impeccable. Use its critique/design intelligence to find generic AI signatures, weak hierarchy, sloppy type, spacing problems, interaction deficiencies, broken responsive behavior, theming inconsistencies, or UX-writing problems. Apply corrections that improve craft while \*\*preserving the candidate's unique thesis\*\*. Do not let Impeccable normalize five different worlds into five variants of Impeccable. Disagreement is useful: surface it rather than averaging it away.

Inspect every candidate in the running browser at representative desktop and mobile widths. Correct obvious collisions, overflow, unreadable contrast, broken controls, missing states, and layout failures before asking the human to judge taste. A broken prototype is not a fair aesthetic candidate.

Maintain a compact decision record:

\*\*Project truths → References inspected → Explored → Critiqued → Human selection pending\*\*

Finish Phase 1 with a Design Tournament evidence packet containing:

1\. the five working preview locations;  
2\. a compact comparison matrix showing each direction's aesthetic thesis, reference basis, signature move, intent fit, and principal trade-off;  
3\. which Taste v2 process shaped each direction;  
4\. where Emil's prototype/design-engineering process was used;  
5\. the most consequential Impeccable findings and which were accepted or rejected;  
6\. responsive/browser status;  
7\. the one decision the human must make next.

Then stop. Do not choose on the user's behalf, generate final hero assets, blend the candidates, or prematurely polish a favorite. Ask which direction should survive and whether any specific element from another candidate should migrate with it.

The outcome of this Phial is not “a finished website.” It is something more valuable at this stage: \*\*five credible worlds, enough evidence to compare them intelligently, and a deliberate human decision about which world deserves expensive refinement.\*\*
